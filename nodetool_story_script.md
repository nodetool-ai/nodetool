# The Thread That Became a Film

**Short film script · approximately 2 minutes**

**Characters**
- **MAYA** — a creator with a deadline and a half-formed idea.
- **ARI** — her collaborator, joining by video call.

**INT. MAYA'S STUDIO — NIGHT**

A blank monitor. A clock reads 11:47 PM. MAYA stares at a page titled *The Lighthouse Project*.

**ARI (ON CALL)**
We promised them a film by morning. What have we got?

**MAYA**
A sentence: “A lighthouse keeps shining after its keeper disappears.”

She opens NodeTool and starts a project. The sentence becomes a brief. A storyboard appears beside it.

**ARI**
That's not a film.

**MAYA**
Not yet.

Maya adds three shots: an empty coast at dawn; a keeper's coat hanging by the door; a beam sweeping across dark water. She creates a keeper character and a lighthouse location, each with a description and reference image.

**MAYA**
If the keeper appears again, I want the same person. If we return to the lighthouse, I want the same place.

Stills arrive in the storyboard. In one, the coat is bright red. Maya pauses.

**ARI**
That wasn't in your sentence.

**MAYA**
No. But it's better.

She updates the character description. The red coat becomes part of the story.

**INT. LIGHTHOUSE — NIGHT — ON SCREEN**

The beam circles. On its next pass, we see a small boat far offshore.

**MAYA (V.O.)**
Every night, someone kept the light burning. Then one night, no one was there.

Back at her desk, Maya places generated clips on a timeline. She shifts the empty doorway before the boat, leaving a beat of silence between them.

**ARI**
Wait. The story isn't about where the keeper went.

**MAYA**
It's about who noticed the light was still on.

Maya writes a final shot: a child in the boat looking up. She plays the sequence. The lighthouse beam finds the child's face. Cut to black.

The clock reads 12:06 AM.

**ARI**
We made a film from one sentence.

**MAYA**
We found the question inside it.

**FADE OUT.**
